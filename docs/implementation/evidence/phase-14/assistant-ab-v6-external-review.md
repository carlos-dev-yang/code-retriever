## Matching gate assessment

Both reviewers agreed without factual qualification that:

- the required-hash mechanism passed: 24/24 reads supplied the hash and
  succeeded, with zero omission, invalid request, or recovery cycle;
- correctness preservation failed: treatment had one unsupported material
  claim and one complete-to-partial reversal;
- the paired token gate failed: median 0.977 exceeds 0.85 and 6/11
  non-increasing is below the fixed count of 8;
- lower aggregate model-total and uncached sums are descriptive only;
- the V4–V6 sequence must stop without V7; and
- the result does not generalize beyond the frozen repositories, questions,
  model, and controls.

## Matching loss classification

Both classified `rhf-x03-form-submit` as final claim/evidence discipline, not
retrieval, ranking, wrong-target navigation, or source-acquisition failure.
The first search found the correct file, the required submission-flow group
was covered, and seven relevant spans were cited. The partial grade came from
an extra `control.handleSubmit` validation/transformation claim whose
implementation was neither read nor cited.

This classification prevents two opposite overstatements: the partial result
cannot be used to call FTS broken, and successful navigation cannot be used to
call the final answer correct.

## Matching orchestration assessment

Both found the 12/12 mechanical-adherence measure necessary but too narrow to
imply efficient orchestration. It proves the explicit search count, hash,
range, and duplicate rules were followed. It does not bound distinct reads,
post-evidence inspection, or unsupported final claims.

The decisive counterevidence is 24 successful reads, only 3/12 tasks stopping
at reducer-detectable complete cidx evidence, 14 later inspection actions, a
nine-read outlier, and higher cidx calls/source volume than V5. Literal
protocol compliance and minimal investigation are different properties.

## Prioritized future directions

The matching recommendations, normalized into three separately scoped items,
are:

1. Product/host claim discipline: require each material final mechanism or
   contract claim to correspond to actually observed and cited source, or be
   omitted/marked uncertain.
2. Product/host stopping discipline: treat evidence completion and subsequent
   source inspection as an explicit budgeted state instead of relying on the
   current search-count and duplicate prohibitions.
3. Evaluation design: keep locator quality, source acquisition, stopping, and
   final claim correctness as separate outcomes; preserve unsupported-claim
   and post-complete-action measurements rather than collapsing them into
   mechanical adherence.

These are not approved implementation changes and do not reopen this series.
An optional rather than mandatory cidx-first policy is a plausible product
direction to evaluate later, but neither review treated it as already proven
efficient.

## Prohibited claims

The external reviews agree that V4–V6 must not be cited as proving:

- consistent model-token efficiency or the frozen 0.85/8-pair gate;
- correctness preservation in the final experiment;
- production-grade claim discipline or minimal orchestration;
- that aggregate token sums override paired correctness/consistency;
- that the V6 partial was a retrieval/ranking failure;
- that 12/12 mechanical adherence implies efficient source use; or
- generalization beyond this exposed panel and execution configuration.

## Final review decision

External review is complete. Close V4–V6 with no efficiency claim, preserve
the bounded locator observation without promoting it, and leave any optional
tool policy, host claim/stopping control, or new evaluation as a separate
